Research Experience

PhD Researcher, April 2022 – March 2026
Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU) Trondheim, Norway

  • Identified 11 celiac disease risk loci by running SAIGE mixed-model GWAS on 17 million variants in ~55,000 participants (~1,100 cases) from the HUNT biobank, correcting for case-control imbalance and relatedness.
  • Strengthened data quality by applying sample and variant QC to 25 million imputed variants (22 million retained), using KING relatedness estimates and principal components to control population stratification.
  • Prioritised candidate causal signals by fine-mapping and colocalising the 11 loci, highlighting a non-coding RNA locus near IRX1 suggesting shared immune-mediated genetics with rheumatoid arthritis.
  • Stratified celiac disease risk by building a Bayesian PRS-CS polygenic risk score (benchmarked against PRSice and LDpred), achieving an AUC of 0.83 in a held-out test set.
  • Characterised genetic heterogeneity within celiac disease by comparing diagnosed and undiagnosed cases, utilizing LDSC to assess genetic heterogeneity between groups.
  • Developed and validated predictive machine learning models for undiagnosed celiac disease in HUNT4, comparing elastic net logistic regression, random forest, SVM, neural networks, and Super Learner ensembles (elastic net selected).
  • Integrated and harmonised real-world data across unique IDs from HUNT surveys, HUNT Biobank, the Norwegian Patient Registry, and clinical journal records to derive ICD-based celiac phenotypes, clinical histories, and causal estimates.
  • Automated end-to-end GWAS workflows with Snakemake on HUNT Cloud for reproducible analysis, and built an open-source R package migrating legacy health-analytics models into version-controlled, Dockerised pipelines.
  • Piloted HLA-region imputation with CookHLA and a containerised Michigan Imputation Server to extend HLA coverage in the cohort.
  • Prototyped a modular R/Shiny tool for Nordic HTA QALY-shortfall analysis across five Nordic countries, reducing ad-hoc requests from external partners.
  • Mentored two master’s students informally, collaborated with molecular biologists and clinical researchers, and secured competitive funding.
